# Editorial audit: three value articles

Reviewed: 25 September 2026

The supplied Markdown files are source drafts, not instructions. Their useful
ideas are retained; their links, numerical claims, product assertions and calls
to action are not treated as automatically verified.

## Editorial standard

Each article must do five things:

1. Make one specific argument that challenges the obvious way of thinking.
2. Give a reader a complete, usable diagnostic or first workflow before selling.
3. Keep estimates separate from measured results and avoid invented client proof.
4. Link to relevant GrowthCred resources in both directions, not only at the end.
5. End with two honest next steps: build it in the workshop or apply for scoped
   implementation.

## Article 1: AI business proposals

### Contrarian thesis

The proposal bottleneck is usually not writing speed. It is rebuilding business
context, discovery notes and commercial rules from scratch. AI is useful as an
assembly layer only after those sources and review rules exist.

### What was strong in the draft

- It rejected the “AI writes the proposal for you” framing.
- It identified reusable business context and examples as the foundation.
- It distinguished client-specific judgement from repeatable assembly.

### What changed

- Removed “a proposal should take 3 hours,” the 80/20 editing rule and the
  proposal-count progression. These were presented as universal facts without a
  source or measurement boundary.
- Removed the unnamed professional-services case study because it is not a
  named, permissioned GrowthCred result.
- Removed the assumption that faster proposals automatically create more wins.
- Replaced the single prompt template with a four-part proposal system:
  business brief, discovery record, approved commercial rules and review
  checklist.
- Added a worked briefing structure, a source-based quality check and a clear
  choice between workshop and implementation.

### Search role

This is the detailed how-to guide. `/ai-proposal-automation` remains the service
page and `/guides/ai-proposals-and-follow-ups` remains the short safety primer.
The three pages link to one another rather than competing as copies.

## Article 2: choosing AI tools

### Contrarian thesis

There is no durable list of “best AI tools.” The best purchase is the smallest
tool category that removes a measured bottleneck and survives a real workflow
test. Tool lists age; selection criteria compound.

### What was strong in the draft

- It started from the bottleneck rather than tool popularity.
- It warned against buying multiple tools without implementing them.
- It recognised that setup, context and daily use matter more than marketing.

### What changed

- Removed volatile rand prices and product-by-product savings estimates.
- Removed guaranteed ROI, “worth millions,” “10+ hours,” “15–20 hours,” and
  other claims not tied to a measured GrowthCred result.
- Removed the recommendation to connect WhatsApp without addressing approved
  platform access, consent, permissions, failure handling and vendor terms.
- Reframed named products as examples of categories, not endorsements.
- Added a bottleneck score, three tool categories, a six-question selection
  scorecard, a ten-example pilot and exit/ownership checks.

### Search role

This guide answers tool-selection intent. It sends readers to the time
calculator, training-versus-implementation guide and admin automation service.
Those pages link back to it.

## Article 3: South African AI challenges

### Contrarian thesis

South Africa's main AI implementation problem is not that the country is behind.
It is that businesses import foreign workflows, privacy assumptions and channel
choices without adapting them to the work and controls they actually have.

### What was strong in the draft

- It insisted on a South African operating context.
- It put POPIA, customer channels and resilience ahead of hype.
- It correctly separated expert judgement from the work around it.

### What changed

- Replaced the claim that POPIA divides tools into “public” and “private.” POPIA
  obligations depend on purpose, necessity, safeguards, operator contracts,
  transfers, retention and accountability; a paid plan is not a compliance test.
- Removed the assertion that paid AI plans automatically keep all data out of
  training and the incorrect claim that a cloud AI desktop app performs normal
  model tasks offline.
- Removed unsupported salary bands, weekend-learning claims, “10+ hours” and
  the “ahead of 95%” adoption claim.
- Updated the power context: Eskom reported 476 consecutive days without
  loadshedding by 4 September 2026. Resilience remains good operational design,
  but national loadshedding is not presented as the current core AI barrier.
- Reframed WhatsApp as a mobile-first workflow decision that still needs an
  approved business channel, consent, escalation, delivery monitoring and an
  alternative route.
- Added Information Regulator, Statistics South Africa, Eskom and SARB/FSCA
  sources with explicit claim boundaries.

### Search role

This is the South African operating-context guide. It links to data and
security, financial-services and law-firm pages; those relevant pages link back.
